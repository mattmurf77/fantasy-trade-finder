# ADR-022: Preference-led bilateral package construction

Date: 2026-09-20. Status: accepted for owner-authorized trial; release status is separate.

## Context

The product promises preferred buys/sells at acceptable market terms, with a package
the other manager can also want. Optimizing viewer gain or simply changing a value
source does not measure that bilateral objective. Completed Sleeper trades describe
observed exchanges, not rejected alternatives. Available paired in-app outcomes are
too sparse and poorly historically attributed to fit calibrated acceptance heads.

## Decision

Construct focal matches using personal preference, outlook and needs together, price
terms with the existing captured market reference, and score both sides separately.
Rank primarily by the weaker side's support. Keep personal evidence ordinal/bounded
and unknown sources explicitly unknown; do not turn user enthusiasm into an asking
price. Prefer minimal sufficient consideration. Apply shared significance/legality
and comparable-quality diversity as secondary controls. Never truncate the eligible
inventory solely for display quotas.

Initial per-side weights are preference .45, market .35 and team plan .20, with
support above .35 and existing market/authority safeguards. These are provisional
interpretable policy coefficients, not fitted or calibrated probabilities. Preserve
their inputs/versions privately so paired observed decisions can test and replace
them. Keep the current market source on launch: KTC Tradesourced blends remain an
offline alternative until validated independently of the objective change.

## Consequences

A single audited selector replaces the old owner arm and supports immediate rollback.
Undecided cached inventory must be model-aware; historical decisions/proofs must not
be rewritten. Unknown counterpart preferences widen uncertainty but do not eliminate
all otherwise plausible matches. Real two-sided exposure/acceptance/completion data,
not market-balance scores alone, determine success. Cold-start quality and repetition
still require owner review and ongoing outcome analysis.
