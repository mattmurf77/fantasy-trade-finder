Sleeper offer history

Review · 10 November 2026

What 612 real trade offers say about how managers decide, and what the app should build because of it. The offers come from one Sleeper inbox across three dynasty leagues, June 2022 to November 2026: 64 were accepted, about 10%. Each card below gives one finding, the numbers behind it and what to do about it. Every recommendation has its own comment box beside it, and each card has one for the finding itself. Only pooled figures appear here; messages and per-manager numbers stay on the local machine.

Read this first: what the sample can and cannot say

limits

Description

One account's trade history, read from the Sleeper website with league-mates' consent. It is the first sizeable record of how real offers were answered, and it has five limits that apply to every card below.

Observations

  • One proposer. 70% of the offers were sent by the same manager. The data shows how about 30 league-mates respond to him and how he responds to them. It cannot separate "what gets accepted" from "how he proposes".
  • Partial history. Sleeper shows only the latest 50 messages per conversation, so 19 of 38 conversations are cut off.
  • Outcomes are inferred. An offer counts as accepted when a completed league trade between the same managers, with the same assets, appeared within three minutes. That matched 59 of 66 completed trades (89%), so acceptance is slightly under-counted.
  • Market value is a different source from the app's. Offers were priced with DynastyProcess monthly values as raw sums, with no package adjustment. 599 of 612 could be priced.
  • Rapid revisions inflate offer counts. 103 offers were replaced by the same proposer within 15 minutes. The fairer unit is the negotiation thread: 280 of them.

Recommendations

Use these findings to choose which effects to build and measure. Do not copy the percentages into the engine as parameters, and do not fit an acceptance model to this sample.

01

Market value barely predicts a yes

supports the premise

Description

Whether an offer is good for the recipient at consensus market value says very little about whether they accept it.

Observations

0.58Ranking offers by the recipient's market gain is only slightly better than a coin flip (0.50 is chance, 1.00 is perfect). It is 0.60 for offers sent to league-mates.

Recipient's result at marketOffersAccepted
Loses more than 30%2237%
Loses 10–30%7712%
Within 10% of even847%
Wins 10–30%8211%
Wins more than 30%13317%
  • Only 10% of accepted trades were within 10% of even at market.
  • In 40% of accepted trades, the manager who said yes lost more than 10% at market.
  • The typical offer asked the recipient to lose 10%.
  • Offers to league-mates reached 21% acceptance only when the recipient won by more than 30%. Buying a yes with value alone is expensive.

Recommendations

R7Aim the opening offer at the other manager winning

Stop treating "fair at market" as the target for a suggestion. A near-even offer was accepted no more often than a lopsided one.

Starting value: the other manager wins by 10–20% at market, then let fit and their own rankings do the rest.

R11Lead card copy with fit, not value

Open each card with why the trade suits the other team, and put the value line second. See card 8 for the reasons people gave.

02

Deals are negotiated, not accepted

first

Description

A single offer rarely closes. Trades happen at the end of a back-and-forth, and the longer the thread, the more often it ends in a deal.

Observations

8% → 57%A first offer was accepted 8% of the time. Threads with five or more offers ended in a trade 57% of the time.

  • Across all 280 threads, 20% ended in a trade.
  • Of 57 completed threads, 35 closed on a later offer, and 15 of those closed on the other manager's counter.
  • After an offer that was not accepted: 44% drew no further offer within a week, 34% were revised by the proposer, and 22% were countered (median 3 hours later).
  • 84% of counters reused at least one asset from the offer they answered.

Recommendations

R2Show the next move with every suggestion

Each card carries a pre-built "if they say no" version that keeps the core assets and adds a sweetener. Counters almost always keep an asset from the offer they answer, so the follow-up can be prepared in advance.

Starting value: one follow-up worth 10–20% more to the other manager, one tap to send.

R10Nudge after silence

Nearly half of unaccepted offers were simply dropped. Prompt the sender with the ready revision instead of leaving the thread to die.

Starting value: 48 hours with no reply.

03

A counter asks for more, by a lot

next

Description

When a manager counters, they are not fine-tuning. They move the deal a long way toward themselves.

Observations

+20 pointsThe median counter moved the trade by 20 points of its value toward the manager countering. 70% of counters improved the counterer's side.

That gap is the best available estimate of how far a typical first offer sits from what the other side wants.

Recommendations

This sets the size for two other recommendations: the opening target in R7 (they win by 10–20%) and the sweetener in R2 (10–20% more). Nothing separate to build.

04

Managers hold their picks

first

Description

A draft pick is worth more to the manager who owns it than the market says. Offers that ask someone to give up picks do far worse than offers that hand picks to them.

Observations

Shape of the offerAccepted
Proposer pays picks for players18%
Pick for pick8%
Proposer asks for picks in return for players7%
  • In the text replies, "not giving up my first" and "holding my draft capital" were among the most common reasons for declining.
  • Player-only offers were accepted 16% of the time, against 9% for any offer involving a pick.

Recommendations

R1Rank "you pay picks" above "you ask for their picks"

When two suggestions are otherwise close, lead with the one where the user's picks go out and a player comes back.

Starting value: a tie-breaker only, so it never promotes a clearly worse trade.

R4Value the other manager's own picks above market

When judging whether the other side would accept, treat picks they own as worth more to them than the consensus price. The app's pick valuation is its strongest advantage, and this is where it should show.

Starting value: about 20% above market for their own picks, smaller for managers who trade often.

Push "several of mine for your best one" suggestions down the deck, and lift suggestions where the other manager comes away with the best asset.

07

Managers differ more than offers do

next

Description

Who receives an offer matters more than anything about the offer itself.

Observations

0% to 36%Acceptance across the 27 league-mates who each received at least eight offers. The median was 9%.

That spread is wider than any offer-level effect on this page.

Recommendations

R8Score how willing each manager is to trade

Use each league-mate's public completed trades per season as a first measure of willingness, and send first-page suggestions toward the managers who trade.

Status: capturing public league history is in a separate pull request (flag off, not yet reviewed). Using it to rank is not built.

08

What people say when they decline

later

Description

17% of unaccepted offers drew a written reply within three days. About 60 of those gave a reason.

Observations

The reasons, in rough order from most to least common:

  • Naming a different player or pick they would move instead (a counter in words).
  • Waiting on an event: a draft slot, an injury report, "after pick 1.03".
  • Attachment to a specific player of their own.
  • Holding their picks.
  • The trade not fitting their team's direction: too old, too long a rebuild, wrong position.
  • Not rating the player being offered.
  • The value being too low. This was the least common stated reason, and two replies said the value was fair and declined anyway.

Recommendations

R9Capture the decline reason in one tap

Offer chips that match what people actually say: "not moving that player", "keeping my picks", "wrong direction for my team", "don't rate what I'd get", "waiting on something", "value too low". Each answer changes what the engine offers that manager next.

R10Re-surface on events

Let a user park an offer until a named event: a pick is made, the draft order is set, or a player's injury status changes.

R11Lead card copy with fit, not value

Value was the reason least often given. The card's first line should be about the other team's need or direction.

09

The app records none of this today

first

Description

Every finding above came from outcomes and counters that live in Sleeper, not in the app.

Observations

  • The app's own data holds fewer than 5 responses from the manager on the other side of a trade, all time.
  • The table meant to hold sent proposals has no rows in production.
  • Without outcomes, none of the starting values on this page can be tuned on more than one person's history.

Recommendations

R3Record what happens to every trade sent from the app

For each trade a user sends: whether it was accepted, declined, countered or left to expire, and what the counter was. This is the measurement every other recommendation depends on.

Re-check: re-run the acceptance evaluation once ten or more managers have responded.
