01

The short answer

Nothing should be removed. Five rules should be rewritten, and three of those must be rewritten together.

1. One rule is 78% of the problem, and you can’t delete it. The junk-filler rule (#141) accounts for 57,815 of the 74,330 trades that any single rule blocks alone. The prosecution showed it killing real starters — Travis Kelce, David Njoku — as “filler.” The defence showed that 91% of what it blocks carries a body worth under 450, and that if you switch it off, every one of the top 200 cards in the deck becomes a padded package, because stapling bodies onto a trade inflates both sides’ gain. Both are right. The rule has two halves: a relative bar (every piece must be 25% of the headliner) that is taxing consolidation, and an absolute floor (450) that is doing its job. Lower the relative bar. Keep the floor. This overturns the first report’s recommendation to lower the 450.

2. The need gate is the one change you can make alone. It’s the only rule with under 26% overlap with the others, so its effect is measurable in isolation, and the fix is already measured (one-sidedness 96% → 89%). Make it a two-sided check instead of a one-sided kill.

3. “Both teams gain” is not enough on its own. The prosecution’s best trades and the defence’s worst trades overlap. A padded package shows enormous gain on both boards precisely because the padding manufactures it. That changes the metric question from earlier today: the right test is both sides gain and nothing in the package is clutter.

Also corrected: the “too-similar” filter is a selector into 5 slots, not a 91% deleter. Loosening it buys near-duplicates, not trades. The real scarcity is upstream — the gates leave ~5.4 distinct ideas per partner.

04

The order, and why it matters

97.6% of rejections are made by two or more rules at once. Four of them punish the same shape on three different value scales. Change them one at a time and you will correctly measure “no effect” — and draw the wrong conclusion.

1

R5 dual-need rescue — alone

Standalone

The only rule whose effect you can see in isolation. One inserted branch; the inputs already exist at both call sites. Ship, measure one-sidedness and partner coverage, then proceed.

2

The consolidation bundle — one change, one measurement

Must move together

filler_min_frac 0.25 → 0.15 (floor held) + trade_elo_gap_max → 0 + R1 repriced in package-adjusted value + v3_shape_max_delta = 2. These nest: #141 would reject 97% of the universe on its own, the gap guard 50%, R1 83%. Loosen one and the next absorbs its recovery. Run the replay harness at 0.15 and 0.10 before flipping; the harness and its validation already exist.

3

R2 → starter-depth predicate

Small absolute numbers (~20 distinct ideas) but the highest mutual gains in the replay. Becomes the next wall once bundle 2 lands.

4

The viewer-must-win decision

Operator

Two forms. Conservative: keep the gate but allow a small loss only when fairness ≥ 0.85 — recovers 1,070 of 1,156, touches nothing below that bar. Full: both-ways + 0.75 floor, which is a different product promise (61% of cards ask the viewer to pay something) and needs card copy. The challenger arm is already serving the full form and is liked 45% vs 23% on that path — let it earn the sample rather than deciding in the abstract.

5

Housekeeping that matters

Audit waiver_slot_cost (425 per extra body, charged before the surplus floor — probably double-counting the depth discount). Make feasibility FLEX-aware. Fix docs/config-reference.md, which still documents the pre-interview filler rule and is wrong about depth-for-depth trades.

What the “remove everything” programme would serve: +71,838 cards, 80% bad by a definition you’d agree to, 75% carrying a body under 450. What the certified-safe programme serves: +434 cards at 15% bad. The volume is not in deleting rules. It is in the full sweep (already building), the 12-asset shortlist, and the shapes the engine can’t build.

05

What this overturns from earlier today

  • First report, R3 (loosen the “too similar” filter to 0.6): withdrawn. The pass selects into a fixed 5–8 slots; loosening it fills the same slots with near-duplicates and recreates #350.
  • First report, R4 (lower the 450 floor to 275): withdrawn. The floor is the half of #141 doing its job; the relative bar is the half taxing consolidation. Both earlier reports had the halves backwards.
  • First report, rule table: the diversity pass graded “High damage” should read “not a gate.” The 8/19 waterfall’s “highest-leverage knob” label on the dual-surplus floor is contradicted by all three lenses.
  • Second-read, the metric question: “like rate when the opponent gains” is still the better compass, but it needs a padding exclusion — both-gain without it rewards the stapled-on bodies the defence documented.
06

How much to trust this

Solid

  • Three analysts worked the same 81,003-row sheet independently and converged on every “keep” and on the #141 split.
  • The defence’s diversity simulation reproduced the engine’s 161 to within 2.5%; the prosecution’s dedupe reproduced the ladder’s 159–161. Independent validation of both.
  • Every code claim cites the current tree; the 8/19 memo’s line numbers have drifted by hundreds.

Shakier

  • One league, six boards, replay not serving. Ratios hold; absolute counts don’t extrapolate.
  • “Blocks alone” is conditional on every other rule at its current value — which is exactly why the bundles exist.
  • The full 3.07M-row candidate file is not in the repo, so multi-rule co-kills were inferred, not inspected.
  • The 0.15 relative bar is the defence’s proposal, not a measured point. Sweep before flipping.

Method: three Opus agents, read-only, each given the replay data, the code, and a distinct brief (convict / rewrite / defend), run in parallel so none could see the others. Reconciled by the lead against the code. No engine line changed.
