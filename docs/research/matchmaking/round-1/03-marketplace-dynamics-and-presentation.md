# Matchmaking Marketplace Dynamics and Presentation

> Round-1 research memo #3. Angle: **two-sided marketplace design, liquidity, fairness, presentation, and product mechanics around the match** in dating apps (Tinder, Hinge, Bumble, OkCupid, Coffee Meets Bagel). Companion memos cover core algorithms and signals/features.
>
> Researched 2026-08-15 from public sources. Confidence flags used throughout:
> **[official]** = platform-documented or peer-reviewed · **[reverse-engineered]** = credible third-party analysis of undocumented behavior · **[speculative]** = plausible inference, weak sourcing.

---

## 0. Framing: the three jobs of any matching market

Alvin Roth (Nobel 2012, market design) gives the canonical checklist for whether a matching marketplace works — it applies to a 12-team dynasty league exactly as much as to Tinder ([The Art of Designing Markets, HBR](http://web.stanford.edu/~alroth/papers/HBR.ArtOfDesigningMarkets.pdf); [What Have We Learned from Market Design?](https://www.nber.org/papers/w13530)) **[official]**:

1. **Thickness** — enough participants and enough potential transactions that good matches exist at all.
2. **Congestion** — thickness backfires if participants can't evaluate enough alternatives in the time available; attention is the scarce resource, and demand concentrates on a few "obviously good" options.
3. **Safety** — participants must feel safe revealing true preferences and transacting *inside* the market rather than gaming it or going around it.

Everything below is dating apps solving these three problems under an extra constraint dating shares with trade-finding: **the transaction requires mutual consent between two parties who each have veto power, and every participant is simultaneously buyer and seller.**

A dynasty league is a *thin* market (10–12 counterparties, fixed) — so the thickness problem is mostly unsolvable and the entire game is congestion management (don't waste the league's scarce attention) and safety (suggestions must be trustworthy enough that people act inside the app). That's why the congestion/presentation literature below is the most transferable part of this memo.

## 1. Liquidity and congestion: attention is the commodity

### What happens with no intervention

- **Demand concentrates catastrophically.** Bruch & Newman's analysis of a large free dating site across four US cities ([Science Advances, 2018](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aap9815)) found a pronounced, city-consistent **hierarchy of desirability** (measured via PageRank on the message graph). Both men and women pursue partners ~**25% more desirable than themselves**, and reply probability falls sharply with the desirability gap. Popular users drown in messages they won't answer; unpopular users get nothing. The "leagues" folk concept is empirically real **[official — peer-reviewed]**.
- **Recommenders amplify it.** Celdir, Cho & Hwang, *Popularity Bias in Online Dating Platforms* ([MSOM 2024](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2022.0132); [summary](https://www.psypost.org/popularity-over-fairness-how-online-dating-platforms-prioritize-users/)), analyzing ~243k users / 30M+ interactions on a major platform: the algorithm recommends popular users significantly more often, **but popular users accept incoming interest less often** — so the extra exposure is partially wasted and total matching is suboptimal. Feeding your scarcest attention (the popular user's) with your highest volume is exactly backwards **[official — peer-reviewed]**.
- **Choice congestion is a modeled, named failure mode** in the platform-econ literature: when preferences correlate (everyone wants the same targets), one-sided ranking "concentrates recommendations on a small subset of receivers, generating severe congestion" ([Managing Congestion in a Matching Market via Demand Information Disclosure, ISR 2022](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/isre.2022.1148); [Integrating Predictive Models into Two-Sided Recommendations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.19689)) **[official — peer-reviewed]**.

### What apps actually do about it

- **Score-and-band ("Elo"/desirability matching).** Tinder for years assigned every user a desirability score updated Elo-style by who swiped on them, and preferentially showed users to others of similar score ([Fast Company, 2016 — Tinder confirmed the score's existence on the record](https://www.fastcompany.com/3054871/whats-your-tinder-score-inside-the-apps-internal-ranking-system)) **[official — confirmed by Tinder, mechanics partly reverse-engineered]**. Tinder announced Elo's retirement in 2019 in favor of "dynamic" activity/recency-weighted scoring, but third-party consensus is that the replacement performs the same function — predicted mutual interest banding and exposure throttling ([profilesharp](https://profilesharp.com/en/blog/tinder-desirability-score-explained), [roast.dating](https://roast.dating/blog/tinder-elo)) **[reverse-engineered]**. Banding is congestion management: it protects popular users from flood and gives everyone counterparties who might actually say yes.
- **Exposure smoothing / popularity dampening.** Multiple credible teardowns report Tinder temporarily *suppresses* profiles receiving too many right swipes and *boosts* under-exposed ones ([PWSkills Tinder system-design teardown](https://pwskills.com/blog/system-design-architecture-of-tinder/), [Cornell Networks course analysis](https://blogs.cornell.edu/info2040/2021/09/30/1-how-tinder-matches-you/)) **[reverse-engineered]**. Academically this is now formalized: counterfactual/IPS-corrected reciprocal recommenders raise long-tail user coverage by up to **51%** and cut Gini exposure inequality by up to **24%** while *improving* predicted match metrics ([CFRR, arXiv 2508.01867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01867)); Nash-social-welfare objectives show fair exposure distribution and total match count are largely complements, not substitutes ([Balancing Fairness and High Match Rates in RRS, ACM TORS](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13609)) **[official — peer-reviewed]**.
- **Rate-limit the greedy side.** Tinder's 2015 right-swipe cap ("Bouncer", ~100 likes/12h) targeted users who swiped right on everyone to harvest matches. Ten days in: **+25% matches per right swipe, +25% messages per match, spam bots down >50%** ([TechCrunch, 2015](https://techcrunch.com/2015/03/12/hate-it-or-love-it-tinders-right-swipe-limit-is-working)) **[official — Tinder-reported figures]**. This is the cleanest public evidence that *restricting* actions improves marketplace health: making likes scarce makes them informative.
- **Force the flooded side to be choosier, structurally.** Kanoria & Saban ([Management Science 2021](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3794); [INFORMS summary](https://www.informs.org/Blogs/ManSci-Blogs/Management-Science-Review/Facilitating-the-Search-for-Partners-on-Matching-Platforms)) prove that in asymmetric markets the platform should **disallow the less-selective side from initiating**, letting the selective/scarce side screen first — and that concealing some quality information helps disadvantaged users without hurting others. This is the theoretical justification for Bumble's women-first rule **[official — peer-reviewed theory]**.

### Transfer to a 10–12 team league

The "popular user" is the contending team with the assets everyone wants; the "starved user" is the rebuilding team nobody messages. Left alone, every suggestion engine will route all 11 teams' proposals at the same 2 rosters. Exposure smoothing translates directly: cap how often any one team appears as counterparty in the league's suggestion stream in a given window, and guarantee every team a floor of viable suggestions per cycle. The Celdir et al. finding warns specifically that flooding the desirable team *lowers* total consummated trades because their accept rate collapses.

## 2. Why apps deliberately do NOT show the "best" match

Several distinct, documented reasons apps withhold or delay the greedy-optimal recommendation:

1. **Sequencing beats greedy — provably.** Rios, Saban & Zheng, *Improving Match Rates in Dating Markets Through Assortment Optimization* ([MSOM 2022](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2022.1107); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3698751)), working with a major US dating platform: choosing each day's assortment to balance *both sides'* like-back probabilities — rather than showing each user their own best options — yielded **~40% more matches than the platform's incumbent algorithm** in simulation and field experiment. A key mechanism: users who recently got matches become choosier (a measured "history effect"), so the platform should meter wins over time rather than front-load them **[official — peer-reviewed + field experiment]**.
2. **Saving good candidates for follow-up.** Torrico et al., *The Dating Heuristic* ([MSOM 2024](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2024.1053); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02584)) give a 1−1/e-guaranteed algorithm whose empirical edge on proprietary app data comes from "careful balancing of initial and follow-up interactions" — i.e., deliberately *not* spending the whole budget of good profiles on day one **[official — peer-reviewed]**.
3. **Restricted choice is a viable product, not a handicap.** Halaburda, Piskorski & Yildirim, *Competing by Restricting Choice* ([Management Science 2017](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2017.2797); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1610187)): a platform that shows *fewer* candidates can charge *more*, because restricting my choices also restricts my rivals' choices — each candidate I do see is more likely to actually be attainable. eHarmony/CMB-style scarcity coexists stably with Tinder-style abundance **[official — peer-reviewed theory]**.
4. **Exploration and portfolio diversity.** Cold-start users have no signal; recommenders must burn some impressions on exploration rather than exploitation, and item-centric exploration literature formalizes routing new/unknown profiles to the users most likely to give informative signals ([arXiv 2507.09423](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09423v1)) **[official — research; app-specific implementations reverse-engineered]**. Teardowns consistently report Tinder mixes bands and injects novelty rather than serving a strict score-sorted queue ([Cornell blog](https://blogs.cornell.edu/info2040/2021/09/30/1-how-tinder-matches-you/)) **[reverse-engineered]**.
5. **Monetization skimming (the cynical reason).** Tinder Top Picks and Hinge Standouts explicitly siphon the highest-predicted-value candidates into paid surfaces ([TechCrunch on Top Picks](https://techcrunch.com/2018/09/11/tinder-launches-its-curated-top-picks-feature-worldwide/); [criticism of Standouts/Roses two-tiering](https://unstar.app/blog/tinder-gold-bumble-premium-hinge-plus-dating-paywalls-2026)) **[official feature, contested intent]**.

**Transfer:** don't dump the single "best" trade for every team simultaneously, and don't rank purely by your fairness/gain score. Meter suggestions over time (history effect: a team that just completed a trade is choosier and busier), keep a reserve of strong suggestions for re-engagement, and diversify the portfolio each team sees (different counterparties, different trade shapes) instead of five variants of the same blockbuster.

## 3. Match-rate vs match-quality, and the incentive tension

- **The paradox is real but the evidence that apps act on it is weak.** The structural tension is undeniable — "every successful match loses two customers" ([Amplitude, the dating app retention paradox](https://amplitude.com/blog/tinder-dating-app-retention-paradox)) — and commentary ecosystems allege deliberate under-matching ([aneu](https://www.aneu.ai/blog/dating-apps-designed-to-keep-you-single), [nexspark](https://nexspark.com/blog/dating-apps-designed-to-keep-you-single)) **[speculative]**. But the best empirical work cuts the other way: Celdir et al. found revenue-maximizing and match-maximizing objectives "not necessarily conflicting" (popular users drive revenue *by matching*, unless they get too selective) **[official]**, and Amplitude's framing — distinguish "good churn" (graduated users) from bad churn, accept cyclical return — is the mainstream product answer **[credible practitioner analysis]**.
- **What platforms demonstrably DO optimize is engagement-weighted matching**, which produces under-matching as a side effect rather than a conspiracy: OkCupid's own published experiments showed the displayed match percentage *causes* behavior — telling 30%-compatible pairs they were 90% matches made them message like good matches ("when we tell people they are a good match, they act as if they are") ([OkTrends "We Experiment on Human Beings", mirror](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/okcupid/weexperimentonhumanbeings.html); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/kashmirhill/2014/07/28/okcupid-experiment-compatibility-deception/)) **[official — platform-published]**. Lesson: the *label* is a lever as powerful as the ranking; platforms holding both can steer volume at will.
- **Hinge is the counter-positioning case**: "designed to be deleted," publishing real-world outcome metrics — the **We Met** feature asks users days later whether the date happened and was good, feeding actual-date ground truth back into ranking ([TechCrunch, 2018](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/16/hinge-is-first-dating-app-to-actually-measure-real-world-success)); Most Compatible picks were reported by Hinge to lead to dates/number-exchanges **8×** more often than standard matches ([VIDA summary of Hinge claims](https://www.vidaselect.com/hinge-most-compatible)) **[official claims, unaudited]**. Hinge grew for years while Tinder shrank, which is at least consistent with quality-optimizing being commercially rational.
- **The engagement-first equilibrium is now visibly failing**: Tinder paying users fell 7% YoY (Q3 2025, 9.2M payers) with multi-quarter declines across Match Group; ~79% of Gen Z report dating-app burnout; swipe fatigue is the industry's own named diagnosis ([Detroit News/AP](https://www.detroitnews.com/story/life/2025/12/09/people-are-dumping-tinder-the-dating-app-wants-to-reignite-its-spark-with-gen-z/87669049007/), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesbusinesscouncil/2025/05/23/swipe-fatigue-trends-reshaping-the-dating-app-industry/)) **[official financials + surveys]**.

**Transfer:** FTF has no perverse incentive — a consummated trade doesn't remove the league from the app; it *creates* re-engagement (rosters changed, new suggestions exist). FTF should unambiguously optimize consummated-trade quality and measure the real-world outcome (was the trade accepted in Sleeper? — the "We Met" analog), because in a league product, successful trades are the retention loop, not churn.

## 4. Presentation and choice architecture

### The evidence on limiting choice

- **More options → worse choices, more regret.** Wu & Chiou 2009: larger profile sets produced more searching, more time on bad options, lower likelihood of picking the best-fit partner ([study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/24144681_More_Options_Lead_to_More_Searching_and_Worse_Choices_in_Finding_Partners_for_Romantic_Relationships_Online_An_Experimental_Study)). D'Angelo & Toma 2017: larger choice sets → lower satisfaction with the chosen partner and more decision reversal, especially when the choice felt reversible ([study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/295093578_There_Are_Plenty_of_Fish_in_the_Sea_The_Effects_of_Choice_Overload_and_Reversibility_on_Online_Daters'_Satisfaction_With_Selected_Partners)) **[official — peer-reviewed]**.
- **Sequential abundance breeds a rejection mind-set.** Pronk & Denissen 2020: as people swipe through a long sequence, acceptance rates *decline ~27% from first to last option* — pure position effect, independent of profile quality ([A Rejection Mind-Set: Choice Overload in Online Dating, SPPS](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1948550619866189)) **[official — peer-reviewed]**. Deck position is destiny: the same candidate shown 40th gets rejected more than shown 4th.
- **One-at-a-time vs deck.** CMB's model (small daily batch, historically "one bagel a day"; men get up to ~21 at noon) is explicitly built on choice-overload research; user-experience accounts confirm the intended behavior change — with few cards, people actually read profiles instead of snap-judging photo #1 ([Global Dating Insights interview](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/interviews/interview-coffee-meets-bagel-doubles-down-on-intentional-dating/), [ponderworthy user analysis](https://ponderworthy.com/coffee-meets-bagel-dating-why-it-actually-works-and-why-it-doesnt-1rex)) **[official design intent + anecdotal outcomes]**. Hinge's Most Compatible surfaces exactly **one** algorithmic pick per day, framed as its best guess (Gale–Shapley-based stable-matching machinery under the hood), and Hinge attributes large lift to the framing+scarcity combination ([Medium explainer](https://srivatsadavuluriiii.medium.com/how-hinge-is-using-nobel-winning-math-to-cure-your-dating-fomo-0e5485a6d0c9), [VIDA](https://www.vidaselect.com/hinge-most-compatible)) **[official feature; mechanism partly reverse-engineered]**.
- **Halaburda et al.** (§2) shows scarcity is also strategically sound, not just psychologically kind: fewer, mutually-attainable options can be worth *more* to users than infinite reach **[official]**.

### Framing and endorsement effects

- The OkCupid suggestion experiment (§3) is the strongest evidence anywhere that **the platform's stated confidence in a match is itself a treatment**: displayed compatibility moved messaging behavior about as much as actual compatibility **[official — platform-published]**.
- Hinge's Most Compatible "8×" claim compounds algorithm + presentation: being told "this is your best match today" concentrates attention and raises effort on that one candidate **[official claim]**.

**Transfer (this is the crown-jewel section for FTF):** present few trades, one screen at a time, with an explicit endorsement tier ("today's best trade for you" ≫ a scrollable list of 100). Cap the daily/weekly suggestion count per team well below what the engine can generate. Expect position effects: whatever you show 30th is wasted inventory that also trains users to reject. And say *why* it's a good match in one sentence — the label does real work.

## 5. Timing, pacing, and notifications

- **Meter wins over time.** Rios et al.'s history effect (§2): recent matches make users choosier; optimal assortments account for what the user already got **[official]**.
- **Deadlines convert.** Bumble's 24-hour match expiry converts loss aversion into first messages — Bumble has cited **~60% match→conversation** conversion; when Hinge beta-tested match expiration, **conversations per match rose ~50%** in a week ([Quartz, "Dating apps are playing mind games so their users don't have to"](https://qz.com/538155/dating-apps-are-playing-mind-games-so-their-users-dont-have-to)) **[official — company-reported]**. Messaging within the first hours strongly predicts response; after ~72 hours a match is functionally dead ([SwipeStats Bumble data](https://www.swipestats.io/blog/bumble-statistics); [PG Dating Pro on post-match silence](https://www.datingpro.com/blog/post-match-silence-in-dating-apps/)) **[reverse-engineered/aggregated data]**.
- **Notification craft.** Hinge (via Braze) sends a morning push when the daily match is ready and an evening reminder before expiry, A/B-testing time and volume ([Braze Hinge case study](https://www.braze.com/customers/hinge-case-study)) **[official — vendor case study]**. Contextual pushes ("you both love hiking — start there") reportedly hit ~16% open rates vs ~5% generic ([Pushwoosh](https://www.pushwoosh.com/blog/user-engagement-push-notifications/)) **[vendor data]**. The dark side: match notifications are textbook variable-reward mechanics, and this is exactly the machinery now blamed for burnout ([Medium — cognitive-bias teardown of Tinder/Bumble/Hinge](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/how-dating-apps-keep-you-engaged-and-make-you-pay-using-cognitive-bias-e0725f964681)) **[analysis]**.

**Transfer:** a fresh trade suggestion should arrive as an event ("your best trade this week is ready"), not sit in an always-full list; a mutual-interest event should push *both* parties immediately with a nudge to open negotiation; and interest/suggestions should decay or expire — an un-acted-on suggestion from 3 weeks ago is stale inventory (rosters and values moved) and teaches users the list is ignorable.

## 6. Double opt-in: what the mutual-like gate buys

- **Rejection insulation.** The gate makes rejection invisible — you only ever learn about mutual interest; a non-match is silent ([ScienceDirect, agent-based model of swiping-app emotional dynamics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2451958825001903)). This keeps hurt (and retaliation) out of the system and lowers the emotional cost of expressing interest, which increases honest signal volume **[official — research + design consensus]**.
- **Permissioned contact = spam control.** Only matched pairs can message. This single rule is why swipe apps have structurally less harassment-by-inbox than open-messaging sites (OkCupid classic-era women's inboxes being the cautionary tale). Widely cited as Tinder's core innovation ([Hacker News practitioner thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=22407241)) **[design consensus]**.
- **Data quality — with a caveat.** Mutual likes are the cleanest label a reciprocal recommender can get, but the gate also *censors* data (you never observe one-sided likes' counterfactual outcomes), and swipe caps are partly there to keep likes meaningful (§1, Bouncer). If likes are free and unlimited, indiscriminate liking destroys the signal — Tinder's own stated reason for the cap ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/03/12/hate-it-or-love-it-tinders-right-swipe-limit-is-working)) **[official]**.
- **Theory:** Kanoria & Saban formalize why gating and even *hiding information* can raise welfare — unrestricted contact triggers wasteful competition and adverse-selection spirals ([Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3794)) **[official]**.

**Transfer:** FTF's suggest → both-interested → negotiate flow is the correct architecture, validated. Keep the non-interest silent (Team B never learns Team A passed on the trade — critical in a 12-person social group that has to keep playing together). Make expressing interest cheap and low-stakes; make *negotiation* the gated, higher-commitment stage. And rate-limit interest if it's ever free enough to spam.

## 7. First-move mechanics and post-match failure

### The post-match cliff (the industry's dirty secret)

Matches mostly die unconsummated: ~**40%** of Bumble matches expire with no message; only ~half of matches get any conversation; ~70% of conversations stall within 2–5 messages; ~10–20% of matches ever become a date ([SwipeStats Bumble statistics](https://www.swipestats.io/blog/bumble-statistics), [Gitnux swipe statistics](https://gitnux.org/dating-app-swipe-statistics/), [CupidAI aggregate stats](https://getcupid.ai/blog/editorial/dating-app-statistics)) **[reverse-engineered/aggregated — treat exact numbers as directional]**. The match is the *beginning* of the funnel's hard part, not the end. Any product that celebrates the match and abandons the pair afterwards leaks nearly all of its created value.

### What apps do about conversion

- **Assign the first move (Bumble).** Women-first messaging solved women's inbox flooding and forced the scarce side to screen (Kanoria–Saban-optimal, §1). But it also concentrated all activation energy on one side — and in Aug 2026 Bumble **dropped the rule** after 12 years, having found its "Opening Moves" system (prompts either party can answer) increased chat initiation, reply rates, and conversation length ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/12/bumble-ends-women-make-the-first-move-men-messages-whitney-wolfe-herd-identity/), [Techopedia](https://www.techopedia.com/bumble-removes-women-first-messaging-policy-leaving-itself-with-an-identity-problem)) **[official]**. Lesson: *who* moves first matters less than *lowering the cost of the first move for whoever is willing*.
- **Turn-taking nudges (Hinge "Your Turn").** Labeling whose move it is cut ghosting **~25%** in Hinge's early tests ([TechCrunch, 2017](https://techcrunch.com/2017/12/20/dating-app-hinge-rolls-out-a-new-feature-to-reduce-ghosting/embed/)) **[official claim]**.
- **Deadlines** (§5): expiry converts intent into action; Hinge's beta saw +50% conversations/match **[official claim]**.
- **Conversation scaffolding.** Prompts, openers tied to profile content, "you both love X — start there" pushes; CMB and Hinge structure profiles so there is always something concrete to respond to **[official features]**.
- **Close the loop on outcomes.** Hinge We Met (§3) measures date-happened/date-was-good and feeds it back — the only public example of a major app training on the *real* end of the funnel rather than in-app proxies ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/16/hinge-is-first-dating-app-to-actually-measure-real-world-success)) **[official]**.

**Transfer:** expect the same cliff: mutual interest on a trade ≠ negotiated trade ≠ executed trade in Sleeper. Instrument each stage. Scaffold the negotiation (pre-fill the opening offer, name whose turn it is, nudge on stall, provide a one-tap "counter with…" move). "We Met" analog: detect executed trades from the Sleeper transaction log and treat that as the ground-truth label for suggestion quality.

## 8. Fairness and bias

- **Popularity bias** is the dominant documented bias (§1, Celdir et al.). Revenue-optimal policies discriminated *more* against unpopular users **[official]**.
- **Feedback loops in logged data:** past exposure policy determines who appears in training data; collaborative filtering then over-recommends the already-popular, entrenching the hierarchy. Mitigations with published numbers: counterfactual/IPS correction (long-tail coverage +51%, Gini −24% — [CFRR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01867)); multi-objective fairness frameworks ([FAIR-MATCH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01063)); Nash-social-welfare objectives showing fairness ≈ efficiency in reciprocal settings ([TORS](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3793538)) **[official — research]**.
- **Demographic bias:** collaborative filtering absorbs users' revealed racial/age preferences and reflects them back amplified; documented extensively in critiques of dating recommenders ([UW Inquiry+Society overview](https://rikithompson.ds.lib.uw.edu/LivingDigital/the-biases-of-dating-apps/); FAIR-MATCH's motivation section) **[official — research]**. Coffee Meets Bagel drew criticism for over-weighting stated ethnic preferences; Tinder/Hinge removed ethnicity filters in 2020 (widely reported) **[official actions, mixed evidence on effect]**.
- **Key operational insight:** in reciprocal matching, fairness-of-exposure is not charity — it is *throughput*. Starved users churn (thinning the market for everyone) and flooded users disengage. The research consensus post-2023 is that a healthy Gini on exposure is a leading indicator of total match volume **[official — converging research]**.

**Transfer:** the analog of demographic bias is *team-situation bias* — e.g., the engine systematically favoring contenders over rebuilders, or high-value rosters over shallow ones. Track per-team exposure (how often each team appears in others' suggestions) and per-team viable-suggestion count as first-class health metrics of the league marketplace. If two teams haven't received a plausible trade in N weeks, that's a liquidity incident, not a ranking outcome.

## 9. Trust & safety as marketplace hygiene

- **Bad actors poison the pool disproportionately.** Bots/spam degrade the like-signal itself; Tinder's swipe cap cut spam bots >50% as a *side effect* ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/03/12/hate-it-or-love-it-tinders-right-swipe-limit-is-working)) **[official]**. Every fake or dead profile a user encounters devalues all future suggestions ("see who liked you" reveals "full of bots" is a recurring 1-star review theme — [Unstar review analysis](https://unstar.app/blog/best-dating-apps-ranked-by-negative-reviews-2026)) **[aggregated user reports]**.
- **The FTC v. Match Group case** is the canonical antipattern: Match.com emailed non-subscribers "someone is interested in you" notices generated by accounts *already flagged as likely fraudulent*, converting scam signals into subscription revenue; paying users were protected from those accounts, free users were not. Settled in Aug 2025 for **$14M** plus permanent conduct changes ([FTC 2019 complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/09/ftc-sues-owner-online-dating-service-matchcom-using-fake-love-interest-ads-trick-consumers-paying); [FTC 2025 settlement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/match-group-agrees-pay-14-million-permanently-stop-deceptive-advertising-cancellation-billing)) **[official — regulatory record]**.
- Roth's "safety" leg (§0): if users don't trust in-platform signals, they transact outside the platform or game it — the marketplace hollows out even while activity metrics look fine **[official — theory]**.

**Transfer:** FTF's "bad actors" are stale rosters (abandoned teams), un-actionable suggestions, and inflated valuations. A suggestion involving a manager who never opens the app is a bot profile. Detect inactive managers (Sleeper activity data) and downweight/flag them as counterparties; never let the engine dangle interest that can't convert — that's the exact behavior the FTC fined.

## Best practices

1. **Manage exposure as a budget, not a byproduct.** Cap counterparty appearances per window; guarantee an exposure floor for every participant; monitor Gini/coverage of exposure. Evidence: Celdir et al. ([MSOM](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2022.0132)); CFRR +51% long-tail coverage without match loss ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01867)); Tinder's own dampening **[reverse-engineered]** ([PWSkills](https://pwskills.com/blog/system-design-architecture-of-tinder/)).
2. **Optimize the assortment for two-sided accept probability, sequenced over time — not one-sided relevance.** +40% matches in a real field experiment ([Rios/Saban/Zheng](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3698751)); hold strong candidates in reserve for follow-up rounds ([Dating Heuristic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02584)).
3. **Make expressed interest scarce enough to be informative.** Tinder's like cap: +25% matches/right-swipe, +25% messages/match, −50% bots ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/03/12/hate-it-or-love-it-tinders-right-swipe-limit-is-working)).
4. **Show few options, one at a time, with an explicit endorsement.** Choice-overload and rejection-mindset findings ([Wu & Chiou](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/24144681_More_Options_Lead_to_More_Searching_and_Worse_Choices_in_Finding_Partners_for_Romantic_Relationships_Online_An_Experimental_Study); [Pronk & Denissen](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1948550619866189)); Hinge Most Compatible 8× claim ([VIDA](https://www.vidaselect.com/hinge-most-compatible)); restricted choice as strategy ([Halaburda et al.](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2017.2797)).
5. **Keep the double opt-in gate, with silent rejection.** Insulates egos (vital in repeated small-group settings), controls spam, produces clean labels ([Kanoria & Saban](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3794); [agent-based model](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2451958825001903)).
6. **Put a clock on everything post-match.** Expiry/deadlines: Hinge beta +50% conversations per match; Bumble ~60% match→chat ([Quartz](https://qz.com/538155/dating-apps-are-playing-mind-games-so-their-users-dont-have-to)). Stale matches are dead by ~72h **[aggregated]**.
7. **Scaffold the conversation, don't just open the door.** Turn-labels cut ghosting ~25% ([Hinge Your Turn, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/12/20/dating-app-hinge-rolls-out-a-new-feature-to-reduce-ghosting/embed/)); contextual openers triple push open rates ([Pushwoosh](https://www.pushwoosh.com/blog/user-engagement-push-notifications/)); lower the first-move cost for whoever will make it ([Bumble Opening Moves outcome, Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/12/bumble-ends-women-make-the-first-move-men-messages-whitney-wolfe-herd-identity/)).
8. **Train on the real outcome, not the in-app proxy.** Hinge We Met closes the loop on actual dates ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/16/hinge-is-first-dating-app-to-actually-measure-real-world-success)). For FTF: executed Sleeper trades are the label.
9. **Treat pool hygiene as a matching feature.** Remove/downweight inactive or fake counterparties before they burn user trust ([FTC v. Match](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/09/ftc-sues-owner-online-dating-service-matchcom-using-fake-love-interest-ads-trick-consumers-paying)).
10. **Use the platform's endorsement deliberately and honestly.** Displayed confidence changes behavior as much as true compatibility ([OkCupid](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/okcupid/weexperimentonhumanbeings.html)) — a reason-why sentence on each suggestion is a cheap, powerful, and (unlike OkCupid's version) honest lever.

## Antipatterns

1. **Greedy one-sided ranking.** Showing everyone their personal best targets concentrates demand, floods the popular, starves the rest, and measurably lowers total matches ([Celdir et al.](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2022.0132); [ISR congestion paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/isre.2022.1148)).
2. **Infinite feed of mediocre options.** Drives the rejection mind-set (−27% acceptance across a session), decision regret, and eventually category-level burnout — the swipe-fatigue crisis is the decade-scale bill for this pattern ([Pronk & Denissen](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1948550619866189); [Forbes swipe fatigue](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesbusinesscouncil/2025/05/23/swipe-fatigue-trends-reshaping-the-dating-app-industry/)).
3. **Celebrating the match and abandoning the funnel.** 40–50% of matches never get a message; ~70% of conversations die in 2–5 messages. The match animation is not the product ([SwipeStats](https://www.swipestats.io/blog/bumble-statistics)).
4. **Dangling interest that can't convert.** Match.com's flagged-scammer "someone likes you" emails → FTC suit, $14M, brand damage ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/match-group-agrees-pay-14-million-permanently-stop-deceptive-advertising-cancellation-billing)). Softer versions: blurred "likes you" teasers full of bots/dead profiles ([review analysis](https://unstar.app/blog/best-dating-apps-ranked-by-negative-reviews-2026)).
5. **Paywalling the best matches.** Top Picks / Standouts two-tiering reads as the platform withholding the product's core promise for ransom; heavily cited in negative reviews and churn narratives ([paywall teardown](https://unstar.app/blog/tinder-gold-bumble-premium-hinge-plus-dating-paywalls-2026)).
6. **Letting a single visible score stratify the community.** Tinder's Elo created a self-reinforcing caste system and became a PR liability the company spent years disavowing ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/3054871/whats-your-tinder-score-inside-the-apps-internal-ranking-system)). Whatever internal desirability/quality score exists, don't expose it and don't let it hard-partition who can see whom.
7. **Deceiving users about compatibility.** OkCupid's experiment worked — which is exactly why it caused a firestorm when disclosed ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/kashmirhill/2014/07/28/okcupid-experiment-compatibility-deception/)). Endorsements must be real or trust in every future suggestion collapses.
8. **Optimizing raw engagement in a matching product.** Variable-reward notification loops and engagement-first ranking produced the burnout, user decline (Tinder payers −7% YoY), and Gen-Z exodus the industry is now living through ([Detroit News/AP](https://www.detroitnews.com/story/life/2025/12/09/people-are-dumping-tinder-the-dating-app-wants-to-reignite-its-spark-with-gen-z/87669049007/)). Match-product engagement must be a byproduct of match value, or it cannibalizes the franchise.
9. **Concentrating all first-move burden on one side by ideology rather than market structure.** Bumble's women-first rule was right for 2014's spam problem and wrong once the binding constraint became conversation-starting energy; it took 12 years to reverse ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/12/bumble-ends-women-make-the-first-move-men-messages-whitney-wolfe-herd-identity/)). Design initiation rules from the current asymmetry (per Kanoria–Saban), and revisit them.

## What matters most (ranked)

1. **Every participant keeps getting viable matches (exposure floors + dampening).** This is the survival condition of a tiny market: in a 12-team league, two starved teams is 17% of the marketplace churned, and their exit thins the pool for the other ten. All the congestion research says throughput and fairness-of-exposure rise together ([CFRR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01867), [TORS Nash](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13609)). Highest leverage, hardest to retrofit.
2. **Two-sided accept probability as the ranking objective, sequenced over time.** The single best-evidenced algorithmic choice: +40% matches in the field ([Rios et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3698751)). For trades: rank by P(A accepts) × P(B accepts), not by value-gap elegance.
3. **Scarcity of presentation (few, endorsed, one-at-a-time suggestions).** Cheap to implement, large behavioral effect, and it compounds with #2 — scarce slots force the engine to spend them well ([choice-overload literature](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1948550619866189); [Halaburda](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2017.2797)).
4. **Post-match conversion machinery (deadlines, turn-taking, scaffolded openings, outcome tracking).** The funnel's biggest leak is after mutual interest; the fixes are proven and cheap (+50% conversations from expiry alone) ([Quartz](https://qz.com/538155/dating-apps-are-playing-mind-games-so-their-users-dont-have-to); [Hinge Your Turn](https://techcrunch.com/2017/12/20/dating-app-hinge-rolls-out-a-new-feature-to-reduce-ghosting/embed/)).
5. **Pool hygiene.** One inactive-manager suggestion costs more trust than ten good suggestions earn ([FTC record](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/09/ftc-sues-owner-online-dating-service-matchcom-using-fake-love-interest-ads-trick-consumers-paying)). In small leagues this is doubly lethal because users can verify the counterparty's inactivity socially.
6. **Honest endorsement/explanation on each suggestion.** Big behavioral lever ([OkCupid](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/okcupid/weexperimentonhumanbeings.html)), near-zero cost, and it differentiates from black-box fatigue.
7. **Double opt-in with silent rejection.** Table stakes — validated architecture; its main value in a small closed league is social-graph protection (nobody learns who passed on them).

## What doesn't matter (even though it seems like it should)

1. **Showing users their globally "best" option immediately.** Feels like the whole point; is actually counterproductive. Greedy assortments lose ~29% of achievable matches vs sequenced two-sided optimization in the field ([Rios et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3698751)), and front-loaded wins make users choosier and harder to serve later (history effect).
2. **More suggestions = more value.** Users given larger sets choose worse, regret more, and reject more as sets grow ([Wu & Chiou](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/24144681_More_Options_Lead_to_More_Searching_and_Worse_Choices_in_Finding_Partners_for_Romantic_Relationships_Online_An_Experimental_Study); [D'Angelo & Toma](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/295093578_There_Are_Plenty_of_Fish_in_the_Sea_The_Effects_of_Choice_Overload_and_Reversibility_on_Online_Daters'_Satisfaction_With_Selected_Partners); [Pronk & Denissen](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1948550619866189)). CMB built a durable business on ~1–21 cards/day; Tinder's infinite deck is the burnout machine.
3. **Compatibility-score precision.** OkCupid demonstrated the *displayed* number drives behavior nearly as much as the true one ([OkTrends](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/okcupid/weexperimentonhumanbeings.html)); Hinge's 8× lift is match-*attention* + framing as much as model accuracy. A well-framed decent suggestion beats an unframed optimal one. (Other memos cover algorithm quality — the marketplace lesson is that presentation captures a huge share of the value of accuracy.)
4. **Which side moves first, per se.** Bumble's 12-year signature rule turned out to be droppable with *improved* conversation metrics once first-move cost was lowered for everyone ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/12/bumble-ends-women-make-the-first-move-men-messages-whitney-wolfe-herd-identity/)). What matters is matching the initiation rule to the market's current asymmetry ([Kanoria & Saban](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3794)) and minimizing first-move friction — not the identity of the mover.
5. **Fairness vs. efficiency being a tradeoff.** Intuition says dampening popular users sacrifices matches; the reciprocal-matching literature repeatedly finds fair exposure *increases* total matches because it routes attention where it can convert ([TORS Nash](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13609); [CFRR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01867); [Celdir et al.](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2022.0132) — popular users' accept rates collapse under flood).
6. **The perverse-incentive conspiracy ("apps keep you single on purpose").** Widely believed, structurally plausible, but the best evidence says revenue and matching aren't fundamentally opposed ([Celdir et al.](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2022.0132)), and the engagement-first apps are the ones *losing* users ([Detroit News/AP](https://www.detroitnews.com/story/life/2025/12/09/people-are-dumping-tinder-the-dating-app-wants-to-reignite-its-spark-with-gen-z/87669049007/)) while outcome-oriented Hinge grew. And it's moot for FTF: executed trades create re-engagement rather than churn — FTF gets to optimize the thing dating apps are accused of sandbagging. **[analysis; incentive claims speculative on all sides]**

## Not researched / follow-up topics

- **Small-pool matching literature specifically (kidney exchange, school choice in small districts, NRMP couples).** The formal thin-market work (match *chains*, batching cadence for thin markets) likely has direct analogs for multi-team/3-way trade suggestion timing; this memo stayed on dating apps per scope.
- **Multi-party matches (3-way trades).** Dating is strictly pairwise; kidney-exchange cycles/chains are the right literature for whether/when to suggest 3-team trades in a thin league. Not covered.
- **Monetization mechanics' effect on marketplace health (boosts, super-likes, paid visibility) in depth.** Touched on Top Picks/Standouts criticism only; a dedicated look at whether paid boosts distort matching (and Experimental-econ "Propose with a rose" signaling work) would sharpen any future FTF premium design.
- **Quantitative benchmarks for notification frequency/caps.** Vendor case studies (Braze, Pushwoosh) gave directional numbers; a proper survey of push-frequency vs churn curves in social apps wasn't done.
- **Repeated-game / social dynamics of closed communities.** Dating apps are mostly anonymous strangers; a dynasty league is a repeated game among acquaintances (reputation, grudges, collusion norms). League-specific social-dynamics research (e.g., on trade vetoes, collusion accusations) was out of scope but is probably the biggest un-mapped risk area for FTF.
- **Voice-of-user evidence on trade-suggestion tools specifically** (KeepTradeCut, Fantrax trade blocks, league-mate finders): how existing fantasy tools handle liquidity/pacing was not researched here (competitor teardown territory).
- **Agent-based simulation of small-league trade markets.** The swiping-app ABM literature ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2451958825001903)) suggests simulating a 12-team league with heterogeneous accept thresholds before tuning exposure caps; not attempted.
- **Legal/consumer-protection boundaries for engagement mechanics** beyond the FTC-Match case (dark-pattern rules, EU DSA implications for recommender transparency).
