Rationale

  • One chooser, not two (#232): the RankHome cards and the Rank-tab sheet currently describe the same flows under different names ("Quick set" vs "Tap players into tiers", "Trios" vs "head-to-heads"). The proposal is one content model — three primary cards + one disclosure — rendered as either a sheet or a screen, with one name per method used everywhere.
  • Outcome labels beat effort abstractions: FASTEST / MOST PRECISE / MOST CONTROL answers the only question a new user has ("which one should I use?") in their own words; the hands-on meter, duplicate time hints and the WE STEER / YOU STEER axis all required learning the app's mental model first (tenet: zero-context UX).
  • Quick rank is a refinement, not a method: it only operates on tiers Quick set already built, and the app itself offers it at Quick set's finish — listing it as a peer row is the #232 confusion in miniature. It moves into the Quick set card as "Then, if you want: Quick rank".
  • Advanced paths survive, demoted: Pick Anchors, the Tiers drag board and Trends keep their rows behind "More ways to rank" (default collapsed) — simple > complex without deleting power-user routes; 5 cards + 7 sheet rows become 3 cards + 1 disclosure.
  • "Continue — no players this high" over "Skip — no players here" (#233): Continue is the verb users scan for in the primary slot (the exact expectation #233 reports); it states the outcome positionally ("no QBs this high" — the walk moves DOWN the ladder, so lower tiers are still coming) rather than a bare fact; and it lets the redundant Skip hide at 0 selected — two side-by-side buttons that do the same thing is the worse confusion. Skip returns with selections, where it means something different.
