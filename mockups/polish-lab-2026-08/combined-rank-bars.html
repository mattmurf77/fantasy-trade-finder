Combined rank bars — merge consensus + my board into one chart (#248)

Operator ask (LeagueRankings): "Want to explore if there's a good way to just display consensus and my board in one bar graph rather than two. Maybe a color per team."

What's actually on screen today (verified against mobile/src/screens/LeagueSummaryScreen.tsx): there aren't literally two charts side by side. There's one vertical stacked-bar chart and a Consensus / My board toggle (basisRow) above it. Picking a basis re-fires the query (queryKey: ['league-power-rankings', leagueId, basis]) and the whole chart re-renders — bars re-height, teams re-sort, the drill-in panel re-derives. So "two bar graphs" is really "one bar graph you have to flip back and forth to compare" — the real ask is seeing both without toggling. Every variant below assumes the frontend gains access to both bases in the same payload (today's per-basis fetch would need to become a combined fetch or two parallel queries — an engineering dependency, not solved here, flagged honestly rather than hand-waved).

Position hexes are a data encoding, not a style choice (docs/cross-client-invariants.md — QB orange/RB green/WR blue/TE purple, shared across web/mobile/extension). "A color per team" as literally proposed would replace that encoding with an arbitrary 12-hue team palette — losing the roster-shape read (how much of this team is QB vs WR) and risking collisions with the position/tier palettes the design system explicitly forbids (docs/design/design-system.md rule 9, "no new hues"). Three variants below keep the position encoding and solve "one graph" a different way; V3 also shows the team-color idea rendered out honestly as an explored-and-rejected option, not a recommendation.

All three reuse the app's own established "de-emphasize the other board" vocabulary — the exact muted-gray palette (GRAY_SEGMENT: QB→chalk.dim, RB→ink.lineStrong, WR→chalk.faint, TE→ink.line) the drill-in focus state already uses for non-selected teams — instead of inventing a second hue system for "the other board."

Why this isn't the recommendation

Solid team-hue bars are what the operator literally described, so it's rendered here rather than argued away in the abstract. Three problems surface immediately once it's actually drawn: (1) it needs 12 brand-new hues for a 12-team league (more for larger leagues) — the design system's rule 9 is "no new hues," and docs/cross-client-invariants.md only reserves four position hues + eight tier hues, nothing for teams; (2) at 12 teams several of these arbitrary hues sit close enough in hue/lightness to be confused at a glance (bar 3 green vs bar 11 green above), which is exactly the kind of ambiguity the tier-vs-position "bright vs deep" hue rule exists to prevent; (3) it throws away the roster-shape read — you can no longer see that a team is QB-heavy or WR-thin from the bar alone, which is information every other variant keeps. If distinguishing "your" bar from the field is the actual goal, the ice-accent "you" ring already shipped on every variant above does that with zero new hues.

Recommendation

V2 (my-board bar + consensus ghost-tick + delta arrows)

V2 is the one variant that's actually a single bar per team — the literal "one bar graph" the operator asked for — while keeping both signals on screen at once: the bar answers "how good is this roster on my board," the tick answers "where would consensus put it," and the chip answers "is that gap worth noticing." It's also the only variant that fully preserves the position-color encoding and adds new information without adding new hues (the tick reuses the "ice tick" vocabulary already blessed for progress fills and focus rings in docs/design/design-system.md).

V3 (delta chip only) is the pragmatic fallback: if the tick-overlay math (sharing one max scale across both bases, positioning an absolute line per column) is judged too much for a first pass, ship V3 first — it's the same chart that exists today plus one small chip, near-zero geometry risk, and upgradeable to V2 later without touching anything else. V1 (paired bars) is the most literal "both boards, side by side" reading and is worth keeping in the back pocket as an opt-in "compare mode," but shouldn't be the default: 24 sub-bars in a 393pt-wide card is legibly a stretch at 12 teams (it gets worse in 14-16 team leagues, which this league format supports) and reading the position makeup of an 11px-wide segment stack is a real ask of the user's eyes.

The team-per-color toggle explored above is explicitly not recommended: it answers "which bar is which team" (something the numeral + roster-panel tap already answer) at the cost of "what does this roster look like" (position makeup) and a new, currently-nonexistent 12+-hue palette that docs/cross-client-invariants.md doesn't reserve for this and docs/design/design-system.md rule 9 forbids introducing without a documented addition.
