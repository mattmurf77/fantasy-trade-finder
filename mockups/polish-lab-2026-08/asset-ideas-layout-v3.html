Variant B, post-tap (static). Same state, but the chip shares the header row: chip left, Featured trade label pushed right. One row shorter than A, at a cost — the section label leaves the left edge where every other Chalkline tick-label sits, and when the chip is hidden (no history) the label either snaps back left or stays orphaned right; either way the header jumps.

Back-chip placement — the common approach, honestly

  • The convention: back affordances sit at the top left, ABOVE the content they revert. iOS puts Back at the leading edge of the navigation bar, its own strip above the page; Android's app bar does the same. Users reach top-left expecting "take me back one step" without reading. Variant A follows that convention; Variant B is the denser non-standard cousin.
  • Recommendation: Variant A. It costs one 28px row (only while history exists — the row collapses when the stack is empty), keeps the Featured-trade tick-label in the same left-aligned position as every other section label on the screen, and the header never reflows when the chip appears/disappears. B saves that row but makes the header the only right-aligned tick-label in the app and shifts layout on chip show/hide.
  • Header de-named: "Featured trade · Breece Hall" → Featured trade. The pinned player already appears in the pin board directly above and on the Trade-away side of every trade in the window — the header repeat was noise.

Carried forward from v2 (unchanged)

  • Tap a row → it becomes the featured trade; the replaced trade becomes the back target; full per-pin history (reset on pin change); the in-window row is tagged and inert; Dynasty Value Swing verdict is the calc presentation for every trade in the window; #209 give-left/get-right order holds everywhere.
  • The back chip stays part of the FeaturedTradeWindow pattern by default — any surface that swaps a trade into the window gets the same revert affordance; it renders only when history exists.
