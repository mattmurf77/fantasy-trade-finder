League candidates → offer drill-in — #300

"Be sure to plan it forward to actually getting to trades. […] clicking the team shows the player at the position of weakness […] I'm aligned on C. But we should present all of our players at that position somewhere on the page where the 'Offer this player' or something similar is presented next to the player." — operator, docs/feedback/items/300-league-rankings-trade-candidates/ operator-answers-2026-08-11.md §2/§5.2. Decided design (§6): filter to one position P → "Trade candidates" section (teams below league median/average at P, weakest first, reusing League summary tiles) → tap a candidate → drill-in shows the caller's own players at P, each with a trailing chevron "Offer" affordance → "Find a Trade with this team" screen-level button.

The verdict this lab exists to produce

Yes — a trailing chevron fits the post-#299 32pt tile. A minimal ice chevron glyph (no visible text) added to the existing right cluster costs ~20pt of horizontal budget (content width 358pt; right cluster grows from badge+posRank's 112pt to 132pt). The worst-case name row (long name + RK + injury tag + top tier badge) goes from 148pt (~19 chars) free for the name to 128pt (~16 chars) — a real but modest cost, the same class of "acceptable, ellipsis handles it" truncation #299's own width-stress test already signed off on for V1. Tile height stays exactly 32pt; nothing about #299's box model changes.
But a literal, visible "Offer" TEXT label does NOT fit as comfortably. Adding "Offer ›" as a small text+icon (mirroring the app's own "‹ All teams" idiom) costs ~54pt, not ~20pt, because it is a word plus an icon plus its own leading gap, not a bare glyph. Worst-case name budget collapses to 94pt (~12 chars) — "Marvin Harrison Jr." truncates to "Marvin Harri" on the single worst-case row (long name + both micro-tags + the widest tier label "4+ 1sts"). That crosses from "acceptable ellipsis" into "the name is the least legible thing on the row," which #299's own verdict never had to accept. See §6 below for the full measured comparison and the recommended synthesis (chevron-only per row + one instructional line at the section header, which keeps the literal word "Offer" on screen without paying the per-row cost on every player).

Open question the reconstruction surfaces
Median or average? — the operator's own answer says both

Operator-answers §1 Q1 records the decision as "Median/average-relative" — a single answer naming two different statistics. plan.md's rejected-alternatives table also collapses them into one row ("Raw positional total, but relative to league median/average"). The two are NOT interchangeable: with an even-sized league, the median sits between two teams and no team need equal it exactly, while the average is pulled by outliers (one hoarding team massively above the rest drags the bar for everyone else). This lab drew "median" throughout because it is the more common fantasy-analytics convention and resists a single hoarder skewing the whole candidates list — but the operator's answer as written does not pick one, and the two produce visibly different candidate sets and a different empty-state boundary in a real league. This needs a one-word confirmation before build (§8 open questions).

Open questions for the operator
  • Median or average? Operator-answers §1 Q1 says "Median/average-relative" without choosing. This lab drew median throughout (§3 above explains why); the two statistics produce different candidate lists and a different empty-state boundary in an uneven league. Needs one word.
  • Is Variant A's synthesis (chevron-only row + one-time section hint) an acceptable reading of "label 'Offer'"? Or does the operator want the word literally on every row (Variant B, accepting the tighter name truncation) or willing to drop posRank on this one surface to afford it (Variant C)? This is the single highest-leverage sign-off in this lab.
  • Group header copy. This lab renders "YOUR WR" (not bare "WR") in the candidate drill-in so it's unambiguous the roster shown is the caller's own, not the tapped team's — a real UX disambiguation need since the SAME setSelectedId mechanism now renders two different rosters depending on entry path (candidate tap → your roster; normal team-row tap → their roster, unchanged). Not specified anywhere in the decided design; flagging the gap and the fix drawn here.
  • What happens if the position filter changes while a candidate drill-in is open? The candidates section only exists with exactly one core position selected; the general drill-in mirrors posFilter live. If the user changes the filter while looking at "your WRs to offer The Rebuild," does the view fall back to normal (their-roster) drill-in behavior, since it's no longer position-scoped weakness browsing? Not mocked here — a genuine edge case worth a product answer before build, not a mockup guess.
  • Candidates section cap/scroll interaction with the list below it. This lab shows 3 candidate rows above the full ranked list; a 12-team league with many teams below median could make the combined section + list long. Plan.md §3.2 says "show all qualifying teams (a league is ≤ ~14 rows; no pagination speculation)" — carried forward here unchanged, just flagging it stacks with the full list still rendering below, unlike a mode where candidates REPLACE the list.
