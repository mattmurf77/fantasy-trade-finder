#300 round 2 — two competing shapes for “rankings → trade”

Operator, round 2: make the Trade Candidates section collapsed by default with Buyers | Sellers pills — Buyers are teams below the median at the focused position (tap → my players, offer them), Sellers are teams above it (tap → their players, target them), and my own team never appears on either side. Orchestrator’s counter-proposal: no section at all — the ranked list is already sorted by that position, so draw one labelled median divider across it and let the side of the line decide the direction.

Both are drawn here at equal fidelity, on the same league, with the same data. A recommendation is at the bottom of this page. The tile geometry, the 44pt problem, the button naming and the side-by-side question live on page 2; pick-tier value presentation and the secondary states live on page 3.

What Direction 1 costs, drawn

  • Structural duplication is unavoidable, not a drafting mistake. Both pills are contiguous slices of a list that is already on the screen, already in that order. Buyers = the bottom half, Sellers = the top half. Nothing is computed that the list does not already show.
  • Two populations disagree on one screen. The section obeys “never show my own team”; the list below cannot (it is the League rankings — omitting the reader would be a bug). So the same screen shows a league of 11 and a league of 12, ~180pt apart.
  • Collapsed-by-default fights the feedback. #300 says the path to a trade is missing. Making the path a disclosure that defaults shut, below the fold, is a smaller version of the same problem. (It is also new persisted state: another AsyncStorage key, another hydrate, another per-user/per-league record to reset on sign-out.)
  • Caps and scrolling come back. A section listing 6 teams inside a screen that already lists 12 has to decide how many to show, whether to scroll internally (nested scroll inside a ScrollView — the pattern this codebase avoids), or to cap and add a “show all”.
  • It does add one thing the list does not have: an explicit intent toggle. That is a real benefit and is what Direction 2 must answer for. See D2-e.

Why both drill-ins are identical — and what that implies

Once the drill-in shows both rosters (operator question 4), the Buyers/Sellers pill above it is deciding something the drill-in immediately un-decides. The pill answers “which direction?”; the drill-in then shows both directions anyway. The pill’s only surviving job is ordering — which group is expanded first — and the side of the median line does that for free.

This is the strongest single argument against Direction 1 that came out of drawing it, and it only appears once you honour the operator’s own “show both teams’ players” request. If the drill-in were one-sided, the pill would be load-bearing. It is not, because the operator asked for both — correctly, since a real trade needs both sides.

5. Recommendation
Ship Direction 2 — one list, one median divider — with two things borrowed from Direction 1. The divider is the shipped playoff-cutline construction reused verbatim; it adds ~34pt and one element to a screen that already computes everything it needs. Direction 1 adds a persisted collapse state, a segmented control, and a second list whose every row is a duplicate of a row already on screen, in exchange for one saved scroll flick.

Borrow from Direction 1:

  1. The vocabulary. “Buyers” and “sellers” are the operator’s words and they are good ones — keep them as the two side labels above and below the line (drawn here as plain-words variants “Richer at WR — target theirs” / “Shorter at WR — offer yours”; both wordings are shown so the operator can pick. Straight “Sellers” / “Buyers” is shorter but assumes the reader knows which side of the deal they are on).
  2. The counts. The strip’s “6 sellers · 5 buyers at WR” summary is genuinely useful and survives Direction 2 as a caption on the chart card — one line, no control.

Where Direction 1 would still be the right call

If the operator’s real requirement is that the candidate set be narrower than “half the league” — e.g. only teams a full pick tier below the median, or only teams whose weakest starter you can actually beat — then a section is justified, because the section would then contain something the sorted list does not: a filtered set. As specified today (“below the median”) it contains exactly the bottom half, and that is the whole reason to prefer the line. This is the single question worth putting back to the operator.

What stopped working once drawn

  • The universal “never show my own team” rule creates the duplication it was meant to avoid — it forces the section’s population (11) to differ from the list’s (12) on one screen. In Direction 2 the rule is unnecessary and is dropped, not violated: your row is the anchor.
  • Collapsed-by-default and “I can’t get to a trade” are in tension. The #169 precedent is sound for an ancillary projection; the candidate route is the payload of the filter.
  • The Buyers/Sellers pill is un-decided by the drill-in the moment both rosters are shown there, which the operator separately asked for.
  • Neither direction’s median can be labelled without new server surface — see page 3. The median value is computable client-side from the payload already on the wire; the pick-equivalent label for it is not. That is an API change and therefore not a “quick fix” under the bright-line rule.
