The shipped 32pt tile — what actually fits, and what 44pt costs

#299 shipped. PlayerCard.tsx on origin/main @ 53bd19f has denseSingleLine (:74 doc, :122 destructure, :222 branch) and styles.cardDenseSingle = { height: 32 } (:472-475). This page measures against that code, not against last round’s mockup CSS.

Method — and why the numbers moved

Every width below comes from measure-tile.py (in this folder), which loads the same TTFs the app loads — @expo-google-fonts/archivo/{400Regular,600SemiBold} and ibm-plex-mono/600SemiBold from mobile/node_modules — and sums real hmtx advance widths, adding React Native’s letterSpacing after every glyph and the exact paddings/borders from PlayerCard.tsx and components/chalkline/Badge.tsx. Run it with python3 measure-tile.py.

Read the frames as geometry, not as typography. The phone frames on this page render in Arial/Helvetica fallbacks (no external font requests are allowed in this lab), so their on-screen glyph widths are not the app’s. Where truncation matters, the frame shows the string the measurement says will render, baked in literally. Trust the annotations; the pixels are indicative.

Delta vs round 1: round 1 put the baseline name budget at 148pt and the worst-case tier badge at 60pt. Measured: 143.8pt and 63.5pt. Round 1 was ~3% optimistic — not enough to change its chevron verdict, but enough to matter for the marginal “Offer” label call, which is exactly the call this page has to make.

2. The 44pt problem the constraint every proposal must answer

“The moment the tile gets an onPress, 32pt stops being safe. […] the dense branch is one Pressable with accessible: true, so a chevron in rightSlot is invisible to VoiceOver and to Maestro id-selectors unless surfaced as an accessibilityAction.” — #299 implementation agent

Confirmed against source. styles.card sets overflow: 'hidden' (PlayerCard.tsx:388). A nested affordance that tries to buy height with hitSlop or padding is clipped by its own parent — you cannot grow a 44pt target inside a 32pt clipped box. Three ways out; all three are drawn.

Drawn outside the tile so it is visible at all — inside it, the box simply disappears. That is the finding, not a drafting choice: a child of a view with overflow: 'hidden' cannot present a hit area larger than its parent.
Two independent failures. (1) Geometry: the hit area is clipped, so the control is a 32pt (or smaller) target however it is styled. (2) Accessibility + testing: the dense branch’s outer Pressable carries accessible: true, which on iOS collapses the whole subtree into one element — a nested button gets no VoiceOver focus and no testID that Maestro can select (this is Maestro flow-authoring law 3 in mobile/.maestro/README.md, learned on-sim). Rescuing it needs an accessibilityActions entry plus an onAccessibilityAction handler — real work, and it still leaves the target clipped.

Why R-3’s hit area is not clipped (and the one thing build must verify)

overflow: 'hidden' clips a view’s children. The hitSlop here is on the tile’s own outermost Pressable and extends outward into its ancestors: rosterRow (a plain View, styles.rosterRow = margin only), drillList (gap only), then the ScrollView content. None of them clips. So the slop lands in unclipped space — the opposite of R-1, where the slop tries to escape the tile it lives inside.

State this as a build gate, not as a fact. Round 1 was called out for reporting mockup geometry as source truth; this lab will not repeat it in the other direction. RN hit-testing through non-clipping ancestors should behave as described, but it must be confirmed on the simulator — tap 5pt above a row and assert the right row fires — before the 44pt claim is written into the PRD. If it does not hold, fall back to R-2 (grow to 44pt); the horizontal design on the rest of this page is unaffected either way.

Row spacing is load-bearing, not cosmetic

With hitSlop 6/6 and the shipped 4pt rosterRow margin, adjacent rows’ touch areas would overlap by 8pt and taps near a boundary would hit the wrong player. The margin must rise to 12 so the slop regions tile exactly (32 + 12 = 44 pitch, zero overlap). That is the whole cost of R-3, and it is why R-3 is 264pt rather than 216pt — 44pt compliance is never free vertically; R-3 just buys it without giving back the look.

Accessibility, concretely

  • Role. The composed label already exists (PlayerCard.tsx:175-196) and accessibilityRole becomes 'button' automatically once onPress is passed (:199-200) — no change needed.
  • Hint. Add one per direction: “Offers Puka Nacua to Punt Gods” / “Targets Ja’Marr Chase from Gridiron Goons”. The label says what the row is; the hint says what activating it does.
  • No accessibilityActions needed — that machinery exists for rows with a second command. One row, one verb, so it stays unused.
  • Maestro. Selector is the tile’s own testID (league-summary.roster.<player_id>), which already survives the accessible-container collapse because it is on the container. Flow-authoring law 3 is satisfied by construction rather than worked around.
  • WCAG 2.5.8 (Target Size, Minimum, 24×24) is met by the pointer target, which hitSlop defines. VoiceOver’s focus rectangle still draws at 32pt — acceptable, since activation is a double-tap anywhere once focused, not a re-aim.

Recommendation on Variant D

D is viable and D is not recommended. The measurement says yes; the content says no. Both dropped tags are decision inputs on this exact surface:
  • Injury. On the offer side, “which of my WRs do I send?” is materially changed by one of them being Q or IR. On the target side, an injured asset is the classic buy-low. The InjuryBadge was explicitly raised from 9px to the 11px floor by teardown S2 PRD-04 because it is a decision input (PlayerCard.tsx:505-509). Removing it on a trade surface undoes that reasoning rather than the pixel.
  • Rookie. In dynasty, RK is a value signal, not decoration.
  • The tags are conditional. Most rows carry neither, so most rows already have ~197pt of name budget. Paying for a per-row word by deleting data from the minority of rows that have data is a bad exchange.
Recommended instead: keep the tags, use a bare chevron, and put the verb in the group header — once. That is variant A (123.8pt worst case: “Marvin Harriso…”, one character worse than #299’s own accepted truncation) plus the groupsub line drawn on page 1: “Tap a player to offer them to Punt Gods.” The literal word “Offer” stays on screen, named once instead of six times, and no row loses data. If the operator wants the word on every row anyway, D1 is the variant to build — it measures clean, and this note is the argument they are overriding, not a blocker.

And this is the argument that decides Direction 1 vs 2

Once the drill-in shows both rosters, a Buyers/Sellers pill above it is choosing a direction the drill-in then un-chooses. The pill’s only remaining job is deciding which group is expanded first — and which side of the median line the tapped row was on already answers that, for free. The operator’s own “show both” instinct is correct and it is what makes the pills redundant.
