D · Inline chips, no sheet

‹ Prev Next ›

No overlay anywhere. The passed card collapses in the feed into a receipt strip carrying the reason chips, and the next trade is already sitting under it, fully interactive. Nothing is on top of anything — the question is a piece of content the user can answer, ignore, or scroll away from, and scrolling away is a first-class outcome rather than a dismissal. The reason stays answerable for as long as the strip is on screen, which turns the two-tap cost into a two-tap option.

Rationale

This is the shape that best satisfies round‑2’s hardest constraint — declining must stay effortless, guilt-free and invisible — while still asking a question. Nothing about a pass becomes a moment. There is no modal to escape, so there is no way for the flow to feel like a toll booth, and round‑3 §5.2’s “register the decline first, never gate it” is satisfied literally: the write happens on tap 1 and the strip is an artefact of it, not a step before it.

It also buys something none of the modal shapes can: a late answer. The user can pass three cards, notice the strip on the way back up, and label one. And because the receipt persists, the “More…” overflow can carry the rare codes without stealing a primary slot — which is how value_overpay_them and partner stay reachable at all.

The honest weakness: vertical space. A deck that is already tall gains a persistent strip per pass, and on a small phone that pushes the next card below the fold — which risks the one metric we must protect, since round‑2 §1.5 only counts a dismissal as a strong negative when the card was demonstrably viewed. If this ships, the strip must collapse to the hairline after one card-height of scroll, and viewport-visibility logging must land before it, not after.
