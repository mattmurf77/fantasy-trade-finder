E · Sampled quick question

‹ Prev Next ›

Ask rarely, and ask inside furniture that is already on screen. Most passes get exactly today’s “Passed · Undo” toast — the shipped 5‑second one. Occasionally that same toast grows one line and three chips. Nothing new appears, nothing new blocks; the surface the user already learned to ignore is the surface that asks. The design bet: we do not need a reason on every pass, we need enough reasons to route revisions — and a question that fires once per session is a question people actually answer.

Sampling policy — which passes get asked

RuleValueWhy
Cadence1 per session, min 4 passes between promptsRound‑2 §1.3’s frequency cliff is about pushes, but the same shape applies in-app: perceived over-asking is what makes people mute a surface. One ask per session is under any plausible cliff.
Eligibility floorcard must be viewed, dwell ≥ 1.5 sOnly a demonstrably-examined card produces a meaningful reason. This is the same gate that makes the dismissal a strong negative in the first place.
Selectionuniform random among eligible passesLoad-bearing. See the warning below.
Cooldown after ignoreskip the next 2 sessionsTwo ignores in a row is an answer. Back off rather than repeat.
Hard stopnever during onboarding or first sessionThe first deck is where the pass habit is formed; do not put anything in front of it.
Kill switchone flag, default OFFThe prompt must be removable without a build.
The trap this design must not fall into

Do not sample on card properties. It is tempting to ask only on lopsided cards, or only on high-confidence cards, because those feel “informative” — and it would silently bias the reason distribution toward whatever the selector keyed on, producing a taxonomy histogram that describes our sampler rather than our users. If any non-uniform rule is ever used, log the selection propensity alongside the answer so the bias is correctable rather than baked in. This is the same discipline round‑2 §1.2 demands of every logged suggestion (log the propensity, the candidate set, the policy version) — and it is impossible to add retroactively.

Rationale

This is the shape you ship if the priority is “change nothing about the pass.” It respects round‑2’s rule that declining stay effortless and invisible more literally than any other approach here, because on most passes there is nothing to respect — the interaction is unchanged. And it puts the ask inside the one component that is already designed to be ignorable: a toast that leaves on its own.

The reason sampling is defensible rather than lazy is that reasons route, they do not train values (round 3, following de Corte et al. 2021 on confabulated self-report). Routing does not need per-pass coverage — it needs enough labelled declines to know which revision policy to reach for, and a distribution stable enough to tell “mostly price” leagues from “mostly blockers” leagues. At FTF’s N that is a much smaller number than “every pass.”

The honest weakness: per-card routing mostly evaporates. If we only labelled one pass in eight, then seven declines out of eight still route as unknown, and the revision loop — which is the entire reason to collect this — fires on a minority of cards. A sampled prompt is a good instrument for measuring the taxonomy; it is a poor instrument for driving the next suggestion. If the goal is the second thing, sample to learn the distribution, then move to a one-tap always-on shape.
