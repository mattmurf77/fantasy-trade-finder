Rationale — and why this is a thinking tool, not a shipping candidate

The grid is the argument. It shows that the operator’s instinct (two dimensions matter) is right and that the drafted list loses two of the most actionable cells: “price is fine, I just don’t want this player” in both directions. It also shows the price axis alone can never distinguish Revise package from Change assets — and those are the two engine moves that matter most, since only a one-asset perturbation makes the next decline interpretable (Baarslag et al., AAMAS 2016).

As shipped UI, though, this is the worst of the five on the dimension that matters most. Round‑3 §2.9 is explicit: friction on the decline path is paid by someone already leaving, and every shipped taxonomy it surveyed is one short step — DoorDash’s ~dozen options are one screen, LinkedIn is binary, Netflix asks nothing. Two modal steps also risks the thing we least want to damage: round‑2 §1.5 grades dismissed‑after‑viewed as the only trustworthy negative in the whole ladder (weight 3–5 vs 0.5–1 for scrolled‑past), and it is trustworthy only because exposure is controlled. A flow that makes people stop passing does not just cost us reasons — it costs us the negatives themselves.

Keep the grid as the spec for the taxonomy. Ship one of the one-step shapes.
