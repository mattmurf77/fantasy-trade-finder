A · Post-pass sheet

Index Next ›

The conservative shape: the x does exactly what it does today — the pass fires, the deck advances, the row is written — and then a bottom sheet asks an optional question. The two axes are split into two labelled groups so a user never has to collapse “the price is wrong” and “the players are wrong” into one answer. Everything below the fold of the answer is optional; the scrim, the grabber and the Skip control all exit to the same already-recorded pass.

Seven live codes, five engine actions. Classes value / fit / hard constraint / timing / partner / unknown are lifted verbatim from round‑3 §5.2 so the routing policy already written there applies unchanged; the two rows marked as additions are where the operator’s framing genuinely extends it. No approach in this lab ever shows all seven at once — Kleinerman (RecSys 2018) caps concrete items at three, and every shipped taxonomy surveyed in round 3 is short.

Rationale & the risk

Splitting the axes is what makes the mapping possible — a flat list cannot distinguish “revise the package” from “change the assets,” and those are opposite engine moves. Round‑3 §5.2 is explicit that the decline registers first and the picker never gates it, which this shape honours. The cost is the sheet itself: a modal scrim on the cheap action inverts the effort gradient the deck depends on, and round‑3 §2.9 puts it bluntly — friction on the decline path is paid by someone already leaving. If the sheet fires on every pass, expect the pass rate itself to fall, and a suppressed pass is strictly worse than an unlabelled one: round‑2 §1.5 grades dismissed‑after‑viewed as the only trustworthy negative we collect (confidence 3–5 against 0.5–1 for scrolled-past). Ship this shape only sampled (E) or reserved for the branch where the second tap buys a genuinely different engine action (see the recommendation).
