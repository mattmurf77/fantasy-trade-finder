B · Reason as action (one tap)

‹ Prev Next ›

The inversion: the reason chips are the pass. Nothing intercepts anything — the plain x is still there and still fires an unlabelled pass in one tap, exactly as today. Beside it sit three more one-tap pass targets that happen to carry a reason. Cost of a labelled pass: one tap, same as an unlabelled one. There is no sheet, no scrim, no interruption, and nothing to skip — ignoring the row is using the x. The one place a second tap is spent is the “Wrong players” branch, because that is the only branch where the second tap changes which engine action fires.

B2 · Same idea, gesture-native

If the chip row proves too heavy to sit on every card, the identical taxonomy can ride the existing left-swipe instead: drag past the threshold and the card lifts off three reason lanes. Releasing over a lane passes with that reason; releasing over dead space passes unlabelled. Still one gesture, zero added chrome — but the reasons are undiscoverable until the first drag, so it needs a one-time coach mark and it cannot be the only path.

Rationale

Round‑3 §5.2’s rule is “register the decline first, then show the picker; never gate the decline behind it.” This shape satisfies the rule by construction rather than by sequencing — there is no “then,” so there is nothing to gate. It also matches the QJE finding round 3 leans on: what kills low-stakes bargaining is fixed cost per round, not delay — reduce per-round effort, and here labelled and unlabelled cost exactly the same.

The scarce resource is the three slots. Kleinerman (RecSys 2018) caps concrete items at three via Pu & Chen’s overload guideline, and every taxonomy round 3 surveyed is short — Netflix ships none at all, LinkedIn is deliberately binary. So the slots go to the three codes that are both frequent and route to different engine actions, and the fork is spent only where a second tap buys the identified blocker: per Baarslag et al. (AAMAS 2016), a rejection of a package differing by exactly one asset “reveals a lot of information,” while a multi-asset revision teaches nothing.

The honest weakness: a permanent three-chip row is real visual weight on a card that already carries a lot, and it nudges — putting reasons in front of a user who was about to swipe past may manufacture opinions they did not hold. Round 3 is blunt that stated reasons are confabulated anyway (de Corte et al., Health Economics 2021): use them to route the next draft, never to update values.
